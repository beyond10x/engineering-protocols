## Outcome

Adopting a generated protocol is a case of its own (Atlas ADR 0086 § Decision 4). `protocol.adopt/1` is Canon `protocol/1` data whose outcome `adopted` is reached only when one revision of a generated protocol has a `canon diff` report within the organization floor, a passing `canon check` report, and an approval under the capability `protocol.adopt`. That capability is held by a human by default; an allow policy is possible for a generation built only from trusted fragments (§ Decision 4). The case pins the revision it adopts (§ Decision 5).

The shape, from the worked example (Atlas `docs/design/governed-autonomy/generated-protocols.md:109-119`). The two claim ids are proposed here; the protocol-first commit fixes them.

| element | id | what |
|---|---|---|
| artifact | `generated_protocol` | the protocol under adoption, at one revision |
| evidence kind | `diff_report` | `canon diff` of that revision against the floor; its result is `within_floor` or not |
| evidence kind | `check_report` | `canon check` on that revision; its result is `passed` or `failed` |
| claim | `floor.respected` | TRUE on a `diff_report` with result `within_floor` bound to the current revision |
| claim | `checks.pass` | TRUE on a `check_report` with result `passed` bound to the current revision |
| action | `protocol.approve` | requires the capability `protocol.adopt` |
| outcome | `adopted` | requires `floor.respected`, `checks.pass` and an approved `protocol.approve` |

Evaluation reads the two reports as evidence and runs neither tool (`AGENTS.md` § Invariants: no clock, network or model call in evaluation). So the protocol can ship before `canon diff` exists: canon `story:semantic-diff` is `proposed` in the canon store, and a fixture carries a report as input.

Not in this story: a terminal outcome for a generation that is not adopted (ADR 0086 names only `adopted`); the floor construct, imports and `canon diff` (canon https://github.com/beyond10x/canon/issues/5); a fragment library (ADR 0086 § Open).

Pressure test: no element names Git, a pull request or code. A generated `software.change` variant and a generated `incident.response` variant are adopted by the same case.

## Acceptance

The test `adoption_needs_floor_check_and_authority` in `crates/canon-engineering/tests/protocol_adopt_protocol.rs` passes under `task check`. Its fixture is `adoption` in `fixtures/protocol-adopt/`: a `generated_protocol` at revision R1, no evidence, no authority decision, one fixed evaluation instant. It expects:

1. Starting from that fixture, `floor.respected` and `checks.pass` are UNKNOWN and evaluation reports no outcome.
2. Starting from that fixture with a `diff_report` of result `within_floor` and a `check_report` of result `passed`, both bound to R1, `protocol.approve` is approval-required and evaluation reports no outcome.
3. Starting from the state of 2 with an authority decision approving `protocol.approve` under `protocol.adopt`, evaluation reports the outcome `adopted`.
4. Starting from the state of 3 with `generated_protocol` at revision R2 and both reports still bound to R1, `floor.respected` and `checks.pass` are UNKNOWN, not FALSE, and evaluation reports no outcome.
5. Starting from that fixture with a `diff_report` whose result is not `within_floor`, a `check_report` of result `passed` and an authority decision approving `protocol.approve`, `floor.respected` is FALSE and evaluation reports no outcome.
6. `canon check` on `protocols/protocol-adopt/1.yaml` finds no unreachable outcome and no authority bypass.
7. `canon-engineering` lists `protocol-adopt@1` among its built-ins (`crates/canon-engineering/tests/protocol_registry.rs::registry_lists_fetches_and_validates_every_builtin`).
